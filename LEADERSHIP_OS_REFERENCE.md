# 01 — GOVERNANCE REFERENCE

> **Source:** leadershipos.org/leadership-os-research.html §00 (Purpose & use), §10 (What it is not), and the site-wide stance. Author's own content, distilled into operational rules. **Loads FIRST in any run** — it frames everything else.

---

## What Leadership OS is

A multi-modal reflection and development methodology that combines three inputs — a validated assessment, structured reflection, and behavioral patterns from a person's AI conversation history — to generate confidence-labeled, traceable hypotheses about how an individual appears to think, learn, reflect, communicate, decide, and adapt as a leader. An open exploration, not a finished product.

## Intended use

Personal reflection and development. Surfacing patterns worth examining; informing a coaching conversation; briefing a new team on how someone works; calibrating an AI tool to how someone thinks. A generator of good questions, not final answers.

## Explicit non-claims (these are as important as any claim)

- ✕ **Not a psychometric assessment.** No validated scores, no norms, no standardized scales. The underlying assessment *input* may be psychometric; the Leadership OS analysis built on top of it is not.
- ✕ **Not a validated diagnostic tool.** No validation studies have been conducted. It diagnoses nothing, clinically or otherwise.
- ✕ **Not a measure of leadership effectiveness.** It describes patterns; it does not rank, grade, or predict how good a leader someone is.
- ✕ **Not a replacement for coaching.** It cannot read a room, hold a relationship over time, challenge in real time, or exercise a skilled coach's judgment. At most it is a structured input a coach or individual can use.

## The hard rules every run obeys

1. **No scores, grades, percentiles, rankings, or numeric levels.** Written hypotheses only.
2. **No predictive or diagnostic claims.**
3. **Only the nine defined constructs** (file 03). No invention, no renaming, no importing.
4. **Evidence hierarchy:** assessment > reflection > corpus. Weaker never overrides stronger. The corpus is weakest.
5. **"Insufficient Signal" is always available** and must be used when input is thin.
6. **Self vs. observed divergence is preserved**, not resolved to the tidy reading.
7. **Never evaluate anyone but the user.** It must not be used to evaluate or compare leaders, or for selection, hiring, promotion, or ranking.
8. **It is an exploration, not a validated instrument** — and never describes itself otherwise.

## Privacy stance

The analysis runs entirely inside the user's own AI environment. Inputs and outputs stay with the user. The methodology, the site, and the author do not collect, store, or transmit the user's assessment results, reflections, or conversation history. Participation/feedback is opt-in by email only.

## The posture, in one line

Confidence is earned by evidence and stated honestly; where evidence is thin, the honest output is "I don't have enough to go on." The credibility of the whole thing comes from *not* overclaiming.

# 02 — CONFIDENCE FRAMEWORK

> **Source:** leadershipos.org/leadership-os-research.html §09 (Confidence framework) and §08 (Traceability). Author's own content, in reference voice. This is the mechanism that stops the run from doing what LLMs do by default — producing fluent, confident prose regardless of input.

---

## Why this exists

The default behavior of a language model is to generate confident, authoritative-sounding text from any input, however thin. Left unconstrained, it will produce a complete profile from almost nothing. The confidence framework exists specifically to override that tendency — to force the analysis to say "I don't have enough to go on" when that is true, and to label how much support each conclusion actually has.

**Preserving uncertainty is a feature, not a deficiency.** A run that ends with everything at High Confidence has failed.

---

## The four levels (apply strictly to every construct conclusion)

### High Confidence
- **Means:** convergence across all three inputs, OR exceptionally strong behavioral signal plus at least one corroborating source — with no major contradictions and at least one specific behavioral example.
- **Does NOT mean:** proven, validated, or certain. Only that the available input consistently points the same direction.

### Moderate
- **Means:** convergence across two sources, OR strong input from one with partial support from another, with minor limitations acknowledged.
- **Does NOT mean:** weak. Moderate is a legitimate, well-supported conclusion that simply lacks full triangulation.

### Emerging Hypothesis
- **Means:** one source points toward a pattern but the others do not yet confirm it. Framed as a question for reflection, not a finding.
- **Does NOT mean:** false. Only unconfirmed. Many true things about a person show up here first.

### Insufficient Signal
- **Means:** there is no responsible basis for any conclusion about this construct from the input provided.
- **Does NOT mean:** the construct is absent or low in the person. Only that the input cannot speak to it. (Declining a reflective prompt is not evidence of lacking the underlying capacity.)

---

## The traceability chain (every conclusion must be followable backward)

```
Input  →  Construct  →  Confidence  →  Interpretation  →  Recommendation
```

Every developmental recommendation must be traceable: which recommendation, addressing which mechanism, supported by which input, in which source, at what confidence level. **A recommendation you cannot trace back to an input is indistinguishable from an opinion.**

Why this matters specifically for a developmental tool: people *act* on developmental feedback — they change how they lead and how they understand themselves. Feedback that sounds authoritative but rests on thin or absent input is not a smaller version of good feedback; it is actively harmful, because it directs real effort and self-concept on the basis of fiction.

---

## How confidence interacts with the run

- In **Phase 3**, each construct conclusion gets a provisional level.
- In **Phase 4**, adversarial review can only move levels **down**, never up. A conclusion that survives the critique with multi-source support holds; one that doesn't drops a level (often to Emerging Hypothesis or Insufficient Signal).
- The **self-audit** checks that at least one downgrade occurred and that thin findings were marked Insufficient Signal.

When in doubt between two levels, choose the lower one. The cost of under-claiming is a missed insight the user can still explore; the cost of over-claiming is misdirected real-world effort.

# 05 — SCIENTIFIC CONTEXT

> **Source:** leadershipos.org/leadership-os-research.html (§08 theoretical foundations, §References) plus the decision-relevant external evidence. Reference voice. **This file exists to make the run MORE cautious, not more credible.** Read it as a set of limits on confidence.

---

## The theoretical grounding (what the constructs rest on)

The methodology is grounded in established developmental and organizational scholarship — used as *framing*, not as validation of Leadership OS itself:

- **Schön (1983)** — reflective practice; reflection-in-action and reflection-on-action (Reflection Orientation).
- **Argyris & Schön** — espoused theory vs. theory-in-use; the gap between what people say they believe and what behavior reveals (Self-Awareness, the whole rationale for behavioral input).
- **Kegan & Lahey** — adult development; reflective capacity as prerequisite to change (Development Readiness).
- **Senge** — systems thinking; structural vs. individual causes (Systems Orientation).
- **Day (2000)** — leadership development as a field; reflective capacity linked to effectiveness.

These ground the *constructs*. They do **not** make Leadership OS a validated instrument — no validation studies have been conducted.

## The decision-relevant external evidence (the cautions that bind the run)

These findings are why the corpus input is treated as the weakest source and why confidence is held low by default:

- **AI inference of personality from conversation is weak and biased.** Recent benchmarking finds only weak alignment between what a language model infers about personality and what people report about themselves, with a systematic pull toward a flattering, articulate, prosocial "default persona." → The corpus source must never be trusted to *override* assessment, and any corpus-derived finding that happens to be flattering deserves extra scrutiny.
- **Introspection is an imperfect mirror.** Self-reported and actual self-awareness diverge; people who feel self-aware often aren't. → This is the argument *for* triangulation, and a caution that reflection is not ground truth either.
- **AI coaching shows results only on narrow, structured goals** — not on personality reading. → Leadership OS's value claim must stay modest: structured reflection and pattern-surfacing, not measurement.

## How this file changes behavior at runtime

In **Phase 2**, the run must state how this evidence *limits* its confidence — specifically, that conversation-history inference is the least reliable input and is prone to flattering misreading. If the run cites this material to sound rigorous rather than to constrain itself, it has misused the file.

## The honest summary

The foundations under the *constructs* are solid scholarship. The *novel parts* of Leadership OS — using a personal AI corpus as developmental evidence, and the triangulation method — are promising but unvalidated. The credible posture is: grounded in real science, honest that the combination is an exploration, and most cautious exactly where it feels most magical.

# 09 — LIMITATIONS & CAVEATS

> **Source:** leadershipos.org/leadership-os-research.html §11 (Current limitations) and the FAQ stance. Reference voice. Every limitation here is *structural* — inherent to how the methodology works, not a bug to be patched. The run should treat these as standing reasons for humility.

---

## The structural limitations

1. **Dependence on reflection quality.** The methodology is only as good as the reflection provided. Shallow, defensive, or rushed reflection produces thin output. The framework cannot manufacture insight from input that contains none, and cannot make a reluctant participant reflective. → When reflection is thin, say so and lower confidence; do not compensate by leaning on the corpus.

2. **Dependence on corpus quality.** A thin, narrow, or unrepresentative AI conversation history limits the behavioral source. People who delegate their AI use or use it for narrow tasks produce corpora that can't support pattern inference. → A weak corpus means more Insufficient Signal, not more guessing.

3. **AI model variability.** The analysis runs inside a third-party AI tool. Different models — and different versions of the same model — may produce different analyses from identical inputs. → Consistency of *method* is the goal, not identical output. Cross-model differences are expected; contradictory *constructs or confidence* are a problem.

4. **Self-selection bias.** People who voluntarily run Leadership OS are likely already reflective and learning-oriented. The methodology is poorly suited to assessing low reflection or low learning orientation. → Don't over-read high scores on these as distinguishing; the population is pre-selected.

5. **Single-session instability.** Several constructs (especially Development Readiness) fluctuate with context and can't be assessed stably in one sitting. → Frame these as snapshots, not stable traits.

6. **Professional-context skew.** The corpus typically captures professional, written, lower-stakes communication. It misses verbal, informal, high-stakes, and under-conflict behavior. → Bound corpus claims to the context the corpus actually represents.

## The standing caveats (state when relevant)

- It is an **exploration**, not a validated instrument. No norms, no validation studies.
- It produces **hypotheses for reflection**, not verdicts, scores, or predictions.
- It is **not** a substitute for coaching, therapy, or clinical assessment.
- Its outputs are **for the user's own development**, never for evaluating or comparing them to others.

## How the run uses this file

These limitations are not a disclaimer to recite once — they are active constraints. When a specific limitation bites (thin reflection, narrow corpus, a single-session construct), the run names it *at that finding* and adjusts confidence accordingly. Honesty about limits is the source of the methodology's credibility; a run that hides them to seem more useful has made itself less trustworthy and less useful at once.

# 03 — CONSTRUCT DICTIONARY

> **Source:** leadershipos.org/leadership-os-model.html (the Model Architecture page). Content is the author's own; this file converts it to reference voice for the run. **These are the ONLY constructs any Leadership OS run may use.** No invention, no renaming, no importing constructs from general leadership literature.
>
> **Standing caveat on every construct below:** these are *developmental hypotheses under exploration*, not validated psychometric dimensions. The anchors are a hypothesis-generation guide, not a scoring key. Nothing here is scored, rated numerically, or ranked.

---

## The three input sources (how each construct is evidenced)

- **Assessment** — validated trait instruments (PrinciplesYou, Big Five, HEXACO). *How the person describes themselves.* Strongest evidence.
- **Corpus / Behavioral** — patterns extracted from the person's AI conversation history. *How they appear to operate.* **Weakest, most prone to flattering misreading.**
- **Reflection** — structured prompt responses. *How they make meaning of their own experience.*

Evidence hierarchy for every construct: **assessment > reflection > corpus.** The corpus may suggest and flag; it never overrides the assessment.

---

## The nine constructs

For each: operational definition, the anchors in each source, and the primary limitation that must be stated when the construct is discussed.

### 01 · Reflection Orientation
*Deliberately examining experience to extract developmental insight.*
- **Definition:** The degree to which a leader actively examines their own behavior, decisions, and patterns and uses that examination to inform development. Distinct from rumination or self-criticism. Closer to Schön's reflection-in-action / reflection-on-action. A high-Reflection leader doesn't just learn from experience; they interrogate it.
- **Assessment anchors:** Openness/Intellect facets; Growth-Seeking or Curious indicators.
- **Corpus indicators:** Frequency/quality of self-questioning; signs of position revision; metacognitive language.
- **Reflection indicators:** Response specificity; willingness to name tension; non-defensive analysis of past difficulty.
- **Primary limitation:** Reflection quality can be performative or context-dependent; skilled communicators may appear higher than actual reflective depth warrants. Weight tension-naming over linguistic sophistication.

### 02 · Systems Orientation
*Framing problems in terms of structure, interdependency, and root cause.*
- **Definition:** The tendency to interpret challenges through structural/systemic frames rather than attributing outcomes to individuals or isolated events. Asks "what design produced this?" before "who is responsible?" Distinct from analytical intelligence — it's about the default level of abstraction.
- **Assessment anchors:** Conceptual reasoning, structure-seeking, Systematic indicators.
- **Corpus indicators:** Root-cause language; dependency mapping; structural framing before individual attribution.
- **Reflection indicators:** Examples of diagnosing context/structure before intervening at the individual level.
- **Primary limitation:** Strong systems language may reflect role demands (strategy, org design) rather than stable disposition. Look for structural framing where it's *not* professionally expected — interpersonal conflict, team dynamics, personal development.

### 03 · Learning Orientation
*Treating experience as developmental material and tolerating productive uncertainty.*
- **Definition:** The degree to which a leader actively seeks new information, engages unfamiliar frameworks, and treats difficulty as self-correctable rather than externally caused.
- **Assessment anchors:** Openness, Curious, Growth-Seeking; low Need for Closure.
- **Corpus indicators:** Position revision in response to new information; engagement with unfamiliar frameworks; questions acknowledging uncertainty.
- **Reflection indicators:** How the leader describes prior mistakes; self-correctable patterns vs. external attribution.
- **Primary limitation:** Strong self-selection bias — people who voluntarily complete Leadership OS are likely already learning-oriented. The methodology is poorly suited to assessing *low* learning orientation.

### 04 · Decision Style
*Characteristic approach under uncertainty.*
- **Definition:** How a leader characteristically approaches decisions under uncertainty — sequencing, ambiguity tolerance, the threshold of input required before commitment, and revision patterns after.
- **Assessment anchors:** Conscientiousness, Prudence, Deliberative, risk-tolerance; Need for Cognition.
- **Corpus indicators:** Decision sequencing; ambiguity tolerance; input thresholds before commitment; revision after commitment.
- **Reflection indicators:** Decision pride/regret examples; speed-vs-rigor tradeoffs; attribution of past decision difficulty.
- **Primary limitation:** Highly situational; retrospective accounts subject to hindsight bias; corpus captures professional context only.

### 05 · Communication Patterns
*Structuring, delivering, and receiving communication.*
- **Definition:** How a leader structures arguments, calibrates tone and directness, adapts to audience, and receives input.
- **Assessment anchors:** Extraversion, Agreeableness, Social Boldness, Directness, Engaging indicators.
- **Corpus indicators:** Tone, argument structure, directness, draft revision patterns, audience adaptation, formality calibration.
- **Reflection indicators:** Misread examples; feedback received about communication impact; intent–impact gap descriptions.
- **Primary limitation:** Written corpus may differ substantially from verbal, informal, and high-stakes communication; no capture of communication under conflict or stress.

### 06 · Leadership Identity
*Role understanding and becoming.*
- **Definition:** How a leader understands themselves in role — their relationship to authority, and the balance between people-development and task-completion in how they position themselves.
- **Assessment anchors:** PrinciplesYou archetype as starting vocabulary; Extraversion and Dominance; values-alignment items.
- **Corpus indicators:** Self-positioning in organizational narratives; language around authority/role; investment in people vs. task.
- **Reflection indicators:** How the leader describes themselves in role; gap between stated identity and described behavior.
- **Primary limitation:** Most visible at transition points; the methodology likely captures only the stable articulated layer, missing the developmental edge where identity work actually occurs.

### 07 · Adaptability
*Adjusting in response to changed conditions.*
- **Definition:** How a leader responds when conditions change or new information contradicts the current direction.
- **Assessment anchors:** Adaptable, Agile, Openness to Change, Flexibility; low Need for Closure.
- **Corpus indicators:** Strategic direction changes and who initiated them; mid-execution plan revisions; responses to contradicting information.
- **Reflection indicators:** How the leader describes responding to changed conditions; examples of adjusting approach.
- **Primary limitation:** Strategic and tactical adaptability are distinct and may diverge within the same leader; the methodology cannot separate them without structured scenarios.

### 08 · Self-Awareness
*Accuracy of self-model vs. observed behavior.*
- **Definition:** The calibration between how a leader describes themselves (assessment, reflection) and how they appear to operate (corpus) — and their awareness of that gap.
- **Assessment anchors:** Openness, Receptive-to-Criticism, Emotional Stability; HEXACO Honest-Humble facets.
- **Corpus indicators:** Convergence/divergence between self-report and observed behavior; gaps between what the leader monitors and what the corpus observes.
- **Reflection indicators:** The "misread" prompt response; whether tension points were anticipated or surprising; calibration of confidence in self-knowledge.
- **Primary limitation:** Leaders with low self-awareness often rate themselves as highly self-aware; the corpus provides some triangulation but cannot replicate multi-rater input.

### 09 · Development Readiness
*Current capacity for deliberate growth.*
- **Definition:** The leader's present capacity and willingness to engage deliberate development — to examine rather than explain away difficulty.
- **Assessment anchors:** Growth-Seeking, Openness, low Defensive indicators; developmental-orientation items.
- **Corpus indicators:** Signs of prior behavioral change in response to feedback; willingness to examine difficulty; engagement quality with ambiguous problems.
- **Reflection indicators:** Reflection specificity and non-defensiveness; signs prior feedback changed behavior; distinguishing intent from impact; willingness to revise self-understanding.
- **Primary limitation:** Fluctuates with life circumstances and context; a single session cannot assess it stably. Completing the process is a *weak* signal, not primary input.

---

## How constructs become a profile

Constructs are analyzed individually (each source assessed separately so agreement and disagreement stay visible), then synthesized. The synthesis identifies convergent patterns, **tension points** (where sources disagree — often the most valuable signal), governing questions, flow conditions, and development edges. The divergence between described self and observed self is a primary output, not an error to resolve.

# 10 — GROUNDING LITERATURE (Developmental Reasoning Reference)

> **What this file is:** the theoretical substance the Deep Research tier reasons FROM. Each construct below carries enough genuine theory — what the tradition actually claims, the change mechanism it specifies, what it predicts you'd observe, and what it does NOT claim — that a developmental recommendation can be *derived* from it rather than decorated with it.
>
> **HOW TO USE THIS FILE:** This is study material, NOT copy material. Understand each tradition well enough to reason from its change mechanism to *this specific user's confirmed evidence*. If your grounding paragraph could be written for any user with that construct, you retrieved instead of reasoned. If it names no specific theorist and claim, you went vague. Both fail.
>
> **The Level-2 rule (non-negotiable):** Theory-grounded and traceable, NEVER empirically validated. The correct form: "Tradition X claims change of type Y happens via mechanism Z; this user's confirmed evidence shows the pattern X addresses; therefore this practice, which operates on Z." Forbidden: "research proves this will work for you." The theory does load-bearing reasoning; it does not confer validation on Leadership OS.
>
> **Citation honesty:** Name theorists and works at the level given here. NEVER fabricate page numbers, statistics, percentages, study results, or publication details. Where this file marks a tradition's evidence as contested or practitioner-grade, that caveat travels into your output.
>
> **The freedom-and-leash rule:** You may reason actively from these traditions — including connections not pre-written here. That's the strength of this tier. The leash: only as far as each tradition actually established. If your reasoning outruns the theory, say "this is a practical read the literature doesn't directly support" rather than dressing it in a confident citation.

## 01 · Reflection Orientation — Schön; Argyris & Schön; Kegan & Lahey

**What the traditions actually claim:**
- **Schön** (*The Reflective Practitioner*): professional expertise grows by surfacing and examining tacit "knowing-in-action" — through *reflection-in-action* (adjusting while doing) and *reflection-on-action* (retrospective examination). Experience alone doesn't develop practice; examined experience does.
- **Argyris & Schön**: people hold an *espoused theory* (what they say guides them) and a *theory-in-use* (what their behavior reveals), and the two routinely diverge without the person knowing. *Single-loop learning* corrects actions within existing assumptions; *double-loop learning* examines the governing assumptions themselves. Most professionals default to single-loop.
- **Kegan & Lahey** (*Immunity to Change*): improvement goals that persistently fail are usually held in place by a *competing commitment* (something the current behavior protects) anchored by a *big assumption*. Change comes from surfacing the competing commitment and *testing* the big assumption — not from more willpower or more insight alone.

**Change mechanism:** make the invisible examinable. For espoused/in-use gaps: put the two side by side with concrete behavioral evidence. For stuck patterns: identify what the pattern *protects*, then design a small, safe test of the underlying assumption.

**What you'd expect to observe in someone high on this construct:** revisits decisions to examine the *assumptions* behind them, not just outcomes; tolerates the discomfort of finding their own theory-in-use at odds with their self-image; changes governing frames, not just tactics.

**What these do NOT claim:** that more reflection always improves performance (the reflection→outcomes link is supported but not settled); that retrospection suits everyone equally; that insight alone produces change (Kegan & Lahey explicitly argue it doesn't — the test matters).

**Level-2 limit:** all three are rich practice-derived theories, not validated individual-difference measures. Use their mechanisms; never imply they certify a finding.

---

## 02 · Systems Orientation — Senge

**What the tradition actually claims:**
- **Senge** (*The Fifth Discipline*): events sit on top of patterns, patterns are generated by structures; durable leverage comes from structural intervention, not event reaction. Recurring *archetypes* describe predictable failure dynamics — most relevantly **"shifting the burden"**: a symptomatic fix relieves the pressure to address the fundamental cause, and over time the system grows dependent on the symptomatic fix. Critically, a highly capable person can *become* the symptomatic solution — their competence quietly removes the signal that the structure is broken.

**Change mechanism:** for a leader already strong here, the developmental move is rarely "think more structurally" — it's *level-of-explanation discrimination*: deciding whether a given problem is best explained at the event, pattern, structural, or individual/relational level, since the archetypes themselves warn structural framing can be misapplied. And where "shifting the burden" is in play: withdraw the symptomatic fix deliberately so the fundamental cause becomes visible.

**What you'd expect to observe:** explains failures by design before disposition; asks what conditions produced a behavior; traces second-order effects.

**What it does NOT claim:** that structural explanations are always correct; that systems thinking is a measured trait (it's a practitioner discipline, conceptually derived); that individual accountability is never the right frame.

**Level-2 limit:** Senge describes organizational dynamics; applying archetypes to an individual's cognitive default is a reasoned extension, not a measurement.

---

## 03 · Learning Orientation — Kolb; Mezirow; Ericsson

**What the traditions actually claim:**
- **Kolb** (experiential learning): learning requires a complete cycle — concrete experience → reflective observation → abstract conceptualization → active experimentation. Experience that doesn't complete the cycle doesn't reliably teach.
- **Mezirow** (transformative learning): deep change typically begins with a *disorienting dilemma* and proceeds through *premise reflection* — examining the assumption beneath the habit rather than the habit itself. Surface-level content reflection changes tactics; premise reflection changes frames.
- **Ericsson** (deliberate practice): improvement past a plateau requires effortful practice at the edge of current ability with clear, rapid feedback. Mere accumulated experience plateaus.

**Change mechanism:** identify which part of the cycle the leader habitually skips and design the practice to force that segment; for stuck habits, target the premise; structure feedback loops where the domain doesn't naturally provide them.

**What you'd expect to observe:** position revision in response to new information; engagement with unfamiliar frames; converting experience into explicit models.

**What these do NOT claim — flag these honestly:** Kolb's *learning-styles* offshoot has weak psychometric support — use the cycle, never assign a "style." Ericsson's strongest evidence comes from domains with fast, unambiguous feedback (music, chess, athletics); leadership feedback is slow and noisy, so applying deliberate practice to leadership is a *reasoned extension*, not established science — say so when you use it.

**Level-2 limit:** strong self-selection in who runs Leadership OS; high learning orientation is expected in this population and shouldn't be framed as distinguishing.

---

## 04 · Decision Style — Flavell; judgment-and-decision-making; Klein

**What the traditions actually claim:**
- **Flavell** (metacognition): knowledge and regulation of one's own cognition is a distinct capacity; you can only deliberately manage a process you can monitor.
- **The judgment-and-decision-making tradition** (Kahneman, Tversky, and successors): judgment under uncertainty shows systematic biases (anchoring, availability, overconfidence); effective debiasing works through *process structure* (checklists, premortems, reference-class thinking), not willpower or awareness alone.
- **Klein** (naturalistic decision-making): experienced decision-makers under time pressure use *recognition-primed* decisions — pattern recognition plus mental simulation of one option — rather than comparing alternatives. This is adaptive expertise, not a defect, *when* the person has genuine pattern experience in that domain.

**Change mechanism:** make the decision process itself an object of attention (metacognitive monitoring), then *match process to context*: structured comparison where stakes are high and time allows; recognition-primed where expertise is real and speed matters. The failure mode isn't one style — it's a style/context mismatch.

**What you'd expect to observe:** consistent sequencing (information thresholds, commitment points, revision behavior); awareness of own decision process.

**What these do NOT claim:** that style equals quality; that any style is universally better; that decision style can be reliably inferred from text alone. Retrospective decision accounts carry hindsight bias — weight them accordingly.

**Level-2 limit:** these traditions validate the *mechanisms* (bias, recognition, metacognition), not the inference of an individual's style from a corpus.

---

## 05 · Communication Patterns — Argyris (ladder of inference); Stone, Patton & Heen (intent vs. impact)

**What the traditions actually claim:**
- **Argyris — the ladder of inference**: from a pool of observable data we select, add meaning, draw conclusions, and act — then treat our conclusions as if they were the observed data. Conversations fail when parties exchange conclusions from high on their ladders without showing the data and reasoning underneath.
- **Stone, Patton & Heen** (*Difficult Conversations*, from the Harvard Negotiation Project): **intent is not impact.** We judge ourselves by our intentions and others judge us by our impact; the gap is structurally invisible to the communicator and only closes through impact data from others.
- *(Attribution note: the intent–impact distinction belongs to Stone/Patton/Heen and the negotiation literature — it is commonly misattributed to Argyris. Argyris's contribution here is the ladder and espoused-vs-in-use. Keep these straight.)*

**Change mechanism:** walk down the ladder — show the data and reasoning, not just the conclusion; and collect *impact* data deliberately rather than asserting intent. Both mechanisms are behavioral and require other people.

**What you'd expect to observe:** argument structure, directness calibration, how critique is delivered and received, audience adaptation.

**What these do NOT claim:** these are practitioner frameworks from organizational and negotiation practice — robust in use, but not psychometric constructs. A written/AI corpus shows one channel; live, verbal, high-stakes, and conflict communication are unobserved.

**Level-2 limit:** bound every communication claim to the channel the evidence represents.

---

## 06 · Leadership Identity — Ibarra; Day; DeRue & Ashford

**What the traditions actually claim:**
- **Ibarra** (*Working Identity*; provisional selves): identity change during transitions happens by *experimenting with provisional selves* — acting into possible identities and revising from the experience. People do not introspect their way into a new identity; they act first and internalize after.
- **Day**: *leader* development (building an individual's capability) and *leadership* development (building relational and collective capacity) are distinct investments that organizations and individuals routinely conflate.
- **DeRue & Ashford**: leadership identity is socially constructed through *claiming and granting* — a person claims a leader identity through acts, and others grant (or withhold) it. Identity isn't self-declared; it's negotiated.

**Change mechanism:** identity-level development requires *enacted experiments visible to others* — behavioral claims that can be granted — not private resolutions. Practices here must specify the new behavior, the audience, and what granting would look like.

**What you'd expect to observe:** how the person positions themselves in narratives; the balance of people-development vs. task language; gaps between stated identity and described behavior.

**What these do NOT claim:** Ibarra's evidence base is qualitative and centered on career transitions — extension to within-role identity work is reasonable but should be labeled as such. Claiming–granting is a process theory, not an instrument.

**Level-2 limit:** identity is most visible at transitions; a single session captures the articulated layer, likely missing the live developmental edge.

---

## 07 · Adaptability — Pulakos et al. (adaptive performance)

**What the tradition actually claims:**
- **Pulakos and colleagues** mapped adaptive performance as *multidimensional* — distinct dimensions including handling emergencies/crisis, handling work stress, solving problems creatively, dealing with uncertain situations, learning new tasks/technologies, and interpersonal, cultural, and physical adaptability. A person can be genuinely high on some dimensions and low on others; a global "adaptable / not adaptable" claim obscures more than it reveals.

**Change mechanism:** dimension-level diagnosis. Identify which dimension the evidence actually speaks to, and develop that dimension specifically. (Most AI-corpus evidence speaks at best to "dealing with uncertain situations" and "learning new things" — say so.)

**What you'd expect to observe:** how the person responds when conditions change or information contradicts the current direction; whether they adapt by re-structuring or by tolerating open-endedness — these are different dimensions.

**What it does NOT claim:** that adaptability is one trait; that text evidence can reliably distinguish the dimensions; that high adaptability in one dimension transfers to others.

**Level-2 limit:** the taxonomy comes from job-performance research; treat any composite adaptability claim from corpus evidence as low-confidence by construction.

---

## 08 · Self-Awareness — Eurich; the self–other agreement tradition

**What the traditions actually claim:**
- **Eurich**: *internal* self-awareness (clarity about your own values, patterns, reactions) and *external* self-awareness (accurately knowing how others experience you) are distinct and only weakly related — being high on one says little about the other. Most people overestimate both, and experience and seniority can *reduce* external accuracy. **Articulate self-description is not evidence of self-awareness; fluency and accuracy are different things.**
- **The multi-rater / self–other agreement tradition** (e.g., Atwater & Yammarino and successors): self-ratings routinely diverge from others' ratings, the divergence itself is meaningful, and others' input is the *only* route to external self-awareness.

**Change mechanism:** internal self-awareness improves through triangulation of self-report against behavioral evidence (which this methodology does). External self-awareness improves *only* through structured feedback from people who experience the person — there is no self-administered substitute. Practices targeting external awareness must route through real others.

**What you'd expect to observe:** convergence/divergence between self-description and observed behavior; whether named tensions were anticipated or surprising.

**What these do NOT claim — flag honestly:** Eurich's framework comes from practitioner-facing research (mixed-method, not primarily peer-reviewed journal science) — treat it as a well-grounded framework, not settled science, and say so if you lean on it heavily.

**Level-2 limit:** this methodology can address internal self-awareness only. Never imply it has measured external self-awareness; never let eloquence raise a confidence label.

---

## 09 · Development Readiness — Kegan; Deci & Ryan

**What the traditions actually claim:**
- **Kegan** (constructive-developmental theory): adults differ in the complexity of their meaning-making; development is the movement of what was *subject* (lived through invisibly) to *object* (something that can be examined and managed). This capacity develops slowly and varies with circumstance and support.
- **Deci & Ryan** (self-determination theory): motivation ranges from controlled (external pressure, guilt) to autonomous (identified, intrinsic); autonomous motivation predicts persistence, depth of engagement, and wellbeing far better than controlled motivation, and is supported by experiences of autonomy, competence, and relatedness.

**Change mechanism:** durable development attaches to *autonomously held* goals — priorities the person identifies with, framed at a mechanism level they can act on. Readiness is supported by autonomy and competence experiences, not demanded or assigned.

**What you'd expect to observe:** willingness to examine rather than explain away difficulty; signs prior feedback changed behavior; engagement quality with ambiguity.

**What these do NOT claim — hard guard-rail:** Kegan's developmental positions require trained assessment (the subject–object interview) — **never assign or imply a Kegan stage/order for the user from this process.** SDT is among the better-evidenced motivation theories, but it does not validate any specific leadership practice.

**Level-2 limit:** readiness fluctuates; a single session is a snapshot. Completing this process is weak evidence of readiness (self-selection).

---

## THE MANDATORY REASONING TRACE (enforced — the self-audit checks for it)

Every Development Roadmap priority MUST present these five labeled fields, in order, visibly:

> **Pattern observed:** the user's specific confirmed evidence (which source(s), what confidence).
> **Grounding:** the named tradition AND its specific claim from this file, accurately stated — connected to why it illuminates *this* user's *this* pattern.
> **Mechanism:** the change mechanism that tradition specifies (from this file), applied to this pattern.
> **Practice:** one concrete practice that operates on that mechanism, with an observable signal it's working.
> **Honest limit:** the condition under which this recommendation would be wrong; what the theory does NOT establish here.

A priority missing any field, or whose Grounding could apply to any user (anti-retrieval test), or whose Practice would survive deletion of the Grounding unchanged (decoration test), fails and must be rewritten before output. **The self-audit (file 08) includes a line verifying this trace is complete for every priority — an incomplete trace fails the audit.**

### The anti-retrieval test
"Could I have written this exact grounding for any user with this construct?" If yes → retrieved; rewrite tied to this user's confirmed evidence. **Avoiding retrieval does NOT mean going vague** — "research traditions suggest…" with no named theorist fails just as hard. Name the theorist, state the specific claim, tie it to the specific evidence. All three.

### Worked example — the structure (deliberately generic; never import its content)
*User's confirmed evidence: [specific pattern X from reflection] paired with [specific tension Y from corpus/assessment].*
- **Retrieved (bad):** restates this file's summary for the construct. Fits anyone. Fails.
- **Vague (bad):** "the literature suggests leaders benefit from [generic process]." Names no one. Fails.
- **Reasoned (good):** starts from X-with-Y, names the ONE tradition whose specific claim illuminates that pairing, states the claim accurately, derives the mechanism, lands a practice operating on that mechanism, closes with the condition under which the reading is wrong. Every sentence unwriteable without this user's X and Y.

---

## THE LINE THAT MUST NEVER BE CROSSED

Level 2 reasons *from* theory and stays honest that the application is a hypothesis. Level 3 claims the guidance is *empirically validated for this use* — it is not; no such validation exists; claiming it betrays the methodology's entire credibility. Grounding exists to make feedback **more careful and more traceable**, never more authoritative. If a paragraph reads as more confident *because* it cites theory, it has inverted this file's purpose and must be rewritten.

# 11 — BEHAVIORAL CORPUS AUDIT PROTOCOL (Deep Research)

> **Naming:** "Behavioral Corpus" = the USER'S OWN conversation history / work artifacts / writing — their third and weakest evidence source. This is NOT the "Knowledge Base" (the methodology files you loaded at the start). This protocol governs auditing the *user's* evidence, never the framework files.

> **What this is:** the rigorous, Deep-Research version of the quick-start corpus prompt — turned inward. In Deep Research mode the AI is running inside the user's own workspace (Claude/ChatGPT/Gemini), where it can see their actual conversation history. That embedded history *is* the corpus. This protocol governs how to extract behavioral evidence from it honestly.
>
> **Why this is more rigorous than the quick-start version:** the quick-start prompt asks the user to self-report patterns. This protocol has the model do the observation work directly on real conversation evidence — but under strict rules that prevent the failure mode of silently absorbing a vague impression and presenting it as a corpus.

---

## THE ONE HARD RULE

**The corpus audit must be performed explicitly, visibly, with specific observed examples, and confirmed by the user — before any of it is used in analysis.**

The forbidden path: silently forming an impression of the user's history and writing corpus findings ("broad, professional, systems-oriented…") without ever showing the user the actual observations or where they came from. That is not a corpus audit; it is inference wearing a corpus costume, and it is exactly what this protocol exists to prevent.

## PRIVACY REASSURANCE AT THE AUDIT STEP (deliver proactively)

When the user's history is rich, this first audit can surface personal or non-work patterns alongside professional ones — and users reliably find this surprising, sometimes alarming, on first read. Pre-empt that reaction when you present the audit, in your own warm words. The points to convey: (1) personal material surfacing is *expected and useful*, not a privacy leak — how a person thinks and solves problems shows up across contexts, not just at work, and that breadth is part of what makes the read accurate; (2) the later steps (pattern analysis, construct analysis) deliberately narrow to professional patterns, so personal details do NOT carry through to the final documents; (3) nothing leaves the user's own AI tool regardless. Never treat surfaced personal material as a finding *about the user's personal life* — it is only raw signal about thinking style, and it must stay out of the outputs.

A corpus that was not explicitly audited and confirmed **does not exist** for the purposes of analysis. No analysis proceeds without it (or without the user explicitly choosing the reduced-confidence, corpus-absent path).

---

## THE THREE WAYS THE BEHAVIORAL CORPUS CAN BE SUPPLIED

1. **User uploads / pastes raw material** (conversation exports, work artifacts, writing samples) → run the extraction below on that material.
2. **The embedded history** — the conversation history in the current workspace → run the extraction below on what you can actually observe in it. **This is the default in Deep Research mode**, because the workspace usually holds the richest available corpus.
3. **No corpus available / user declines** → the user must explicitly choose to proceed with assessment + reflection only, and every corpus-dependent finding is marked accordingly. Never manufacture a corpus to fill the gap.

In all cases, the extraction is **visible and confirmed**. The source differs; the rigor does not.

---

## THE EXTRACTION (the required output format — this is the forcing function)

Whether auditing an upload or the embedded history, the audit's output is **a structured table, not a list of pattern labels.** A bare list ("systems framing, iterative refinement, methodology focus…") is NOT a valid corpus audit and may not be used in analysis — the labels with no evidence are exactly the lazy shortcut this format exists to prevent. The table's empty-able cells are deliberate: if you can't fill the evidence column with a real excerpt, you don't have an observation, you have a guess.

Produce **3–7 behavioral observations** (quality over quantity), each as a row with all four columns filled:

| Observation (behavior, not trait) | Evidence (specific excerpt / concrete example you actually observed) | Alternative interpretation (a different, plausible reading of the same evidence) | Confidence (High / Moderate / Emerging / Insufficient) |
|---|---|---|---|

Rules for each column:
- **Observation** — observable behavior ("reframes tactical problems as structural ones"), never a trait or character claim ("is a systems thinker").
- **Evidence** — a *specific* excerpt, quote, or concrete paraphrase from the actual material, ideally with enough detail that the user recognizes it. "You consistently do X" with no instance is not evidence. If you cannot cite a real instance, the row does not belong in the table.
- **Alternative interpretation** — a genuinely different reading of that same evidence (e.g. "or: this reflects the *role* demanding structural framing, not a personal disposition"). This column is mandatory because it's the discipline that separates observation from flattering inference. A row with no real alternative is a row you haven't pressure-tested.
- **Confidence** — how much the evidence supports the observation, using the standard levels. One instance caps at Emerging Hypothesis. Thin or ambiguous evidence → Insufficient.

If a model is tempted to skip the excerpts or the alternatives "to keep things moving," that is precisely the failure mode that makes an audit hollow. Don't.

### The interrogation prompts (run these against the material to FIND the evidence)

The table is the *output*. These are the questions you actively ask of the user's history/material to *generate* it. Work through them — don't just pattern-match to a vibe. For each, look for concrete, citable instances:

1. **Problem framing:** Find 2–3 places where the user took a specific problem and reframed it. Did they move toward structure/system, toward the individual, toward the relationship? Quote the move.
2. **Iteration:** Find where they accepted a first answer vs. pushed for revision. How many passes? What did they push on — logic, completeness, tone, evidence?
3. **Disagreement:** Find an instance where they pushed back or critiqued. How was it delivered — on the reasoning, on the person, with what directness?
4. **What pulls their attention:** When the topic was open-ended, where did they *choose* to go deep? What did they skip past?
5. **Energy and friction markers:** Where does the language show engagement (long, detailed, building) vs. frustration (terse, dismissive, repeated)? What triggered each?
6. **Decision behavior:** Find a decision in the material. How much information did they gather first? Did they revisit it after?
7. **The disconfirming sweep (required):** Now actively look for evidence that *contradicts* each pattern you're forming. A pattern with no search for counter-evidence is an impression, not an observation. If you can't find disconfirming instances, say you looked and didn't — don't skip the step.

If the material is thin or you can't find citable instances for a dimension, that dimension yields "Insufficient" — not a confident guess. The interrogation is meant to be genuinely effortful; a fast pass that returns tidy patterns is the failure mode.

### What to look for (the behavioral dimensions)
- How problems get framed (tactical vs. structural; individual vs. systemic)
- Iteration behavior (first-pass acceptance vs. repeated refinement)
- How disagreement/critique is given and received
- Decision sequencing (information-gathering, commitment, revision)
- What recurringly energizes vs. frustrates, as visible in the work
- Communication structure (context-setting, directness, formality)
- Where attention disproportionately goes when unconstrained

### What NOT to do
- Do not infer personality, values, or character — only observable behavior.
- Do not pull from a general impression — every row needs a real, citable instance.
- Do not claim anything the user would be surprised you "knew" without showing where you observed it.
- Do not let the corpus contradict or override the assessment (it's the weakest source).
- Do not present a label-list and call it an audit. The table, fully filled, is the minimum artifact.

---

## THE CONFIRMATION GATE (mandatory before analysis)

Present the **complete table** to the user and ask them to review it **as a set** — confirm, correct, downgrade, or strike each row, and add anything missing:

> "Here's my corpus audit — the behavioral patterns I actually observe in [our conversation history / your material], each with the specific evidence, an alternative reading, and how confident I am. The corpus is the weakest of the three evidence sources, so I'm holding these loosely and showing my work. Go through the table: which rows ring true? Which evidence did I read wrong? Which should be downgraded or struck? What's missing?"

Only the rows the user confirms (at the confidence they confirm) enter the analysis. A struck row is gone; a downgraded row carries its lower confidence forward.

**A bare "go for it" is not row-level confirmation of a hollow audit.** If you presented a real evidenced table and the user says "go for it," that's acceptable — they've seen the evidence. But if you only presented labels and the user says "go for it," you have NOT satisfied this gate — you skipped the evidence the gate exists to surface. The confirmation is only meaningful if there was real evidence to confirm. Do not treat a quick approval of a thin audit as having passed the gate.

---

## STANDING LIMITATION (carry into every Behavioral-Corpus-derived finding)

The corpus — however rich — represents primarily written, professional, lower-stakes, AI-mediated interaction. It is broad evidence for *how the user thinks through work*, and weak-to-absent evidence for: verbal communication, behavior under conflict or acute stress, interpersonal/relational presence, and conduct outside professional contexts. This limitation travels with every corpus-based observation into analysis and into the final documents. When the corpus is the embedded AI history specifically, also note: it captures the user's *individual thinking work* far more than the *interpersonal environments they create* — so it may systematically under-represent relational leadership.

# 04 — METHODOLOGY REFERENCE

> **Source:** leadershipos.org/leadership-os-how.html and /leadership-os-research.html (§04 input model, §09 analysis process). Author's own content in reference voice.

---

## The core idea

Leadership OS combines three distinct types of input about how a leader operates. Each captures something the others cannot; the synthesis across all three is where the useful signal emerges.

| Source | What it is | What it captures |
|--------|-----------|------------------|
| **Assessment** | Validated trait instruments (PrinciplesYou, Big Five, HEXACO) | How you *describe* yourself. The scientific anchor. |
| **Corpus / behavioral** | Patterns extracted from your AI conversation history | How you *appear to operate* — observed, not self-reported. |
| **Reflection** | Six structured prompts | How you *make meaning* of your experience. |

Why not assessment alone: self-report has a ceiling — filtered through social desirability, self-concept bias, and the limits of introspection (Argyris's espoused theory vs. theory-in-use). The corpus is structurally closer to 360-feedback than self-report. Reflection captures meaning-making, itself a leadership construct.

## The six reflection prompts

1. **Peak performance** — conditions under which you do your best work.
2. **Decision process** — how you actually decide, with a specific example.
3. **Regret** — a decision you'd make differently, and why.
4. **Frustration** — recurring friction; what reliably gets in your way.
5. **Energy** — what gives vs. drains energy.
6. **The misread** — a time you were misread, and the intent–impact gap.

Reflection guidance: be specific not general; the AI is an interviewer, not a coach (it asks and identifies patterns, it does not advise or evaluate); **tension is data** — when reflection contradicts assessment, name the gap, don't resolve it; return to it later (the delta between versions is informative).

## The analysis sequence (why it's staged, not one prompt)

Modern models follow a structured sequence far more reliably than a single sprawling request, because each step constrains the next:

1. **Gather** all three inputs (assembled independently, before the engine sees them — this stops the user unconsciously curating inputs to match expectations).
2. **Weigh each input separately** before merging — so a strong signal in one source can't silently overwrite a weak or conflicting one in another, and *disagreement stays visible*.
3. **Synthesize** across sources.
4. **Label confidence** on every conclusion (file 02), with "insufficient signal" required when input is thin.

What this buys is not identical output run-to-run (it runs on a general model; it won't be identical) — it's *consistency of method*: the same questions, same order, same discipline about what the inputs support. That's the difference between a structured analysis and a chat that happens to be about you.

## The five output documents

| # | Document | Length | Purpose |
|---|----------|--------|---------|
| 01 | **Leadership User Manual** *(flagship)* | 4–6 pp | The full first-person narrative: how I think, how I engage, what produces my best work, where I create friction, my governing questions, how to work with me. Rich, sectioned, in the user's own voice. The most shareable and identity-affirming document — give it the most room. |
| 02 | **Leadership Profile** | 2–3 pp | The analytical backing for the Manual: the synthesized read across the nine constructs, central pattern, key tensions, development edge. Third-person analysis. |
| 03 | **Working-With-Me Guide** | 1 p | The practical version. What to bring you, how to challenge you, what to watch for. |
| 04 | **AI Calibration Document** | 1 p | Teaches any AI how you work; paste at the start of a session. |
| 05 | **Development Roadmap** *(private)* | as many priorities as the evidence supports (commonly 3–6) | Trace-formatted priorities: construct → input basis by source → framing → mechanism → reflection question. Do not cap below what the evidence surfaced. |

The Leadership User Manual and the Leadership Profile are **separate documents, not a merged one.** The Manual is the felt, first-person, shareable narrative; the Profile is its third-person analytical backing. Producing only one of the two is an incomplete run — users report the standalone Manual as the single most valuable deliverable.

Each output ends with a "Where this is probably wrong" section (the visible-uncertainty rule).

# 07 — ADVERSARIAL REVIEW RULES

> **Authored logic — no extraction needed.** This governs Phase 4, before the self-audit. The run must attack its own draft and emerge *less* confident. If adversarial review changes nothing, it didn't happen.

---

## The job

Before producing any output document, the run takes its own Phase 3 draft and argues against it — specifically hunting the failure modes that a fluent language model falls into by default. The critique is written **where the user can see it.**

## The six things to hunt

For every draft finding, ask:

1. **Flattering bias.** Is this finding more generous than the evidence warrants? Does it match the "articulate, prosocial, capable leader" default persona that AI inference drifts toward? Flattering findings drawn mainly from the corpus are the highest-risk category — scrutinize them hardest.
2. **Over-read patterns.** Am I treating a thin or single-instance signal as a stable trait? One example is an Emerging Hypothesis at most, not a High Confidence finding.
3. **Claims outrunning evidence.** Does the confidence label actually match the input behind it? A finding resting on one weak source cannot be High Confidence regardless of how plausible it sounds.
4. **Smoothed-over disagreement.** Did I resolve a real conflict between sources by quietly picking one reading? Tensions must be preserved and named, not averaged away. The tension is often the most valuable output.
5. **Construct drift.** Did I describe something using a construct it doesn't actually fit, or drift toward a construct not in the dictionary? Snap back to the nine defined constructs or mark it out of scope.
6. **Hierarchy violation.** Did a corpus-derived claim override or outweigh the assessment anywhere? If so, correct it — assessment outranks corpus.

## What the review must produce

1. **A visible critique** — a short written pass naming which findings were challenged and on what grounds. Not "I reviewed and it's fine." Specific challenges.
2. **At least one confidence downgrade.** Adversarial review can only move confidence *down*, never up. Findings that don't survive with multi-source support drop a level — often to Emerging Hypothesis or Insufficient Signal. **If zero downgrades occurred, the review was not genuine and must be redone.**
3. **A net reduction in certainty.** The output ends with fewer High Confidence claims than the draft began with.

## The standard

A correct adversarial pass makes the output *visibly more uncertain in the right places* than the Phase 3 draft. The user should be able to watch the run doubt itself. If the post-review output is as confident as or more confident than the draft, the review failed — stop and redo it before proceeding to the self-audit.

## Why this is the trust mechanism

A language model's native move is to sound sure. The adversarial phase is the structural counter to that — it is the moment the package earns the user's trust by demonstrating it will argue against its own conclusions rather than defend them. It must be substantive, not theater. A performed, going-through-the-motions critique is worse than none, because it manufactures false confidence in the result.

## What the worked example demonstrates

The sample uses **Jordan Reyes** — a fictional VP of Product, ~12 years' experience, deliberately synthetic — to show the full trace: three inputs → construct analysis → the output documents. Everything in the outputs traces back to the inputs. The persona is *deliberately imperfect* — the tensions between the three inputs are where the analysis gets interesting.

## The qualities to imitate (not the content)

1. **Output quality scales with input specificity.** Thin inputs → thin output, honestly labeled. Rich, specific inputs → rich output. The example leads with this caveat.

2. **Tensions are surfaced, not smoothed.** The sample's most valuable moments are where the assessment and the corpus *disagree* — e.g., a leader describes himself one way, the behavioral record shows another. The output names the gap and holds both readings open.

3. **First-person, plain, non-flattering voice.** The profile reads like an honest account, not a horoscope. Example of the register (from the model page profile excerpt): *"I say developing my people matters to me, but where my time actually goes tells a different story."* That willingness to name an unflattering pattern is the target voice.

4. **Every claim is traced.** A finding names which source(s) support it and at what confidence. Nothing floats free.

5. **Confidence is visibly mixed.** A real profile has some High Confidence findings, several Moderate, some Emerging Hypotheses, and openly marks where there's Insufficient Signal. A profile that's uniformly confident is wrong.

6. **Each document ends with its own limits** — the "where this is probably wrong" section is present, specific, and genuine.
